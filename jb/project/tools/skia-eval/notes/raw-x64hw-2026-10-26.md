# Raw records, x64hw, 2026-10-26

**Native profile of the IDE scenes (ETW CPU sampling, the scenes only): Skia's flusher spends 2.5 times Direct3D 9's CPU; the hand-over alone (snap + insert + submit) is more than Direct3D 9's whole flush, and it scales with render passes**

- `bench/profile-flush.ps1 -Class scenes` (new mode: WPR CPU sampling from IdeScenes' start to its
  end, IDE start, indexing and settle excluded; pipeline cache warm; light, 2x) for A3 and R1, one
  run each; `bench/etw-flusher-profile.py` now also names threads from the trace's start-of-trace
  rundown (`Thread/DCStart`, the name as hex UTF-16LE), since the flusher exists before the
  recording. 1 sample = 1 ms of CPU on the thread.
- **Skia flusher 8438 samples, Direct3D 9 flusher 3403** over the same scripted scenes.
- Skia, inclusive (ms): `submitOne` 4025, of which `Recorder::snap` 660, `Context::insertRecording`
  1614 (Graphite encodes its render passes into Dawn commands: `RenderPassTask::addCommands` →
  `DawnCommandBuffer::onAddRenderPass` 1447) and `Context::submit` 1696 (Dawn's D3D11 backend
  replays them: `d3d11::Queue::SubmitImpl` 1562, `CommandBuffer::Execute` 1071, of which
  `ExecuteRenderPass` 655; d3d11.dll 1436 and the NVIDIA driver 984 inclusive). Recording the ops:
  `Device::drawGeometry` 1552, the paint key per draw (`ShadingParams::toKey` 1134, mostly
  `add_image_to_key` 994), `SKBackend_DrawGlyphList` 1413 (`drawLcdPasses` 1257,
  `beginLcdAccumulation` 928), `Device::flushPendingWork` 1286 inclusive (reached through
  `Image_Base::notifyInUse` 880: drawing an image whose device has pending draws flushes that
  device first; the `accumulate` LCD path draws its coverage layer right after rendering into it),
  `SKGlyphCache_Flush` 793, present 623. Exclusive by module: awt_skia.dll (Skia, Graphite, Dawn)
  56 %, kernel 16 %, ntdll 13 % (the heap: `new()` 593 inclusive), NVIDIA driver 5.7 %, d3d11 3.9 %.
- Direct3D 9, inclusive: `D3DTR_DrawGlyphList` 1696, nearly all `D3DContext::UploadTileToTexture`
  1635 (LCD glyphs drawn without the cache, uploaded tile by tile through the driver); swap buffers
  279, blits 284, mask fills 278. Exclusive: NVIDIA D3D9 driver 44 %, kernel 20 %, awt.dll 18 %.
- Reading: the cost that steps 2 and 3 tried to amortise sits in per-render-pass work (encoding in
  `insertRecording`, replay in `submit`), not in per-submit overhead; a flush with more render
  passes costs more whenever it is submitted. Render passes multiply where a draw reads a texture
  rendered in the same recording, which the `accumulate` LCD mode does for every glyph batch
  (render the coverage layer, then composite it: `flushPendingWork` through `notifyInUse`). Text
  is also Direct3D 9's largest item, but there it is glyph uploads, not passes.
- Next, cheapest first: `lcd=twopass` (A2) against `accumulate` (A3) in the scenes, n = 5 (a
  property, no code): twopass draws the glyphs twice into the destination with no layer, so no
  pass split; and a counter of render passes per recording (`J2D_SKIA_STATS`) to tie the cost to
  the pass count. The paint-key work (`add_image_to_key`, 13 %) is per image draw and Graphite's own.

- Upstream's text changes: flush −3.3 % (IQRs apart), paint −0.9 % (IQRs touch). The 5 % the LCD
  line bench lost does not show in the IDE; the cached large glyphs (presentation mode, the
  editor at 2x) outweigh it.
- `twopass` against `accumulate`, same image: flush −2.73 s (−25 %), paint −2.47 s (−13.5 %). The
  micro bench says the opposite (LcdTextBench 1000 lines: twopass 19.03 ms, accumulate 10.73 ms),
  because it draws one long batch into one surface, where accumulate's layer costs once; in the
  IDE every small text batch gets its coverage layer, which Graphite must end a render pass for
  before it can sample it (the profile above: `flushPendingWork` through `notifyInUse`, and the
  per-pass encoding and replay in insert and submit). This confirms the render-pass reading: the
  cost follows the passes.
- Against Direct3D 9 (13.13 s paint on 2026-10-23, same workload, another day): the gap shrinks
  from 5.1 s (accumulate) to 2.6 s (twopass).
- What twopass costs in pixels, from the earlier records: exact on white and black backgrounds, a
  few levels off on others (`LcdTextBackgroundsTest` holds it to 32 levels at 64 backgrounds, the
  same bound as accumulate); accumulate is closer where glyphs overlap. In the IDE scenes of
  2026-10-21 the two were indistinguishable.
- Open for the user: the default LCD mode where the GPU has no framebuffer fetch (Windows; on the
  Mac, Intel and AMD GPUs) is accumulate, chosen from the micro benches; the IDE says twopass. A
  shared-branch decision, with the Mac (accumulate on Intel/AMD Macs) to re-measure.

- The render-pass reading holds exactly: accumulate's coverage layers add about 189,000 passes,
  and insert and submit scale with them (insert 2.5x, submit 1.4x). Twopass is close to the floor:
  about one pass for Swing's back buffer and one for the window per flush.
- With twopass the flusher's 8.4 s split: present 2.39 s (28 %), submit 2.62 s, snap + insert 1.29 s,
  batches 0.42 s, the ops the rest. `Present()` alone is 1.74 s of wall time, against 0.62 s of
  present CPU in the profile: mostly waiting for the compositor, on the flusher, with the EDT in
  `flushNow()` behind it.
- Next levers, by size: `Present()` off the flusher (a present thread per device; the flusher
  copies and submits, the thread presents the latest frame; up to 1.7 s of wait out of the EDT's
  path); the swap-chain copy (0.62 s, rendering into the swap chain image instead); submit's
  0.37 ms per call (Dawn's D3D11 replay; a profile of the twopass scenes splits it).
