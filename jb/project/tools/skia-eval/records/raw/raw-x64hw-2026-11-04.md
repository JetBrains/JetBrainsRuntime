# Raw records, x64hw, 2026-11-04

**Where the x64hw evaluation stands (Ryzen 5 5600G, RTX 3080 Ti, Windows 11; IntelliJ IDEA 2026.3 EAP on `java-design-patterns`; typing and UI-scene launches, JFR, J2D_SKIA_STATS, ETW). A summary of 2026-10-28 … 2026-11-03 for the ledger, with the judgment asked for**

Numbers are medians of n = 5 interleaved launches unless noted. The latest series (s18) did not include the software
loops, Direct3D 9 and Dawn/D3D12; those rows are from series 1 (2026-11-03 morning), and the IDE's own painting
drifted between series (JBR Vulkan's EDT paint typing 8.04 s in s1, 9.79 s in s18), so across-series rows compare
loosely.

- Typing latency (key press to editor painted) is the same on every configuration: p90 8-10 ms, mean 1.8-2.8 ms.
  The differences are EDT and CPU time (headroom, power), not visible lag in these paced scenes.
- Skia on Vulkan against JBR Vulkan, the flush wait: 2.28x (s1) → 1.53x typing, 1.51x → 1.20x UI (s18). The EDT's
  whole paint: +3 % typing, −6 % UI, since Skia's windows no longer need Swing's back buffer (JBR Vulkan still uses it;
  the same change is open to it).
- GPU against CPU: the EDT paint is 1.5x (typing) and 1.6-1.8x (UI) faster on Direct3D 9 / JBR Vulkan than on the
  loops; the drawing alone (the loops' paint minus the about 6 s of Java-side painting every pipeline does) about 3x
  typing and 4-5x UI. RenderPerfTest (2026-10-28): JBR Vulkan 2.07x the loops where its output agrees.

**Where Skia's time goes against the direct pipelines (ETW 2026-11-03, typing; the profile predates the last two changes)**

- Per draw: Graphite's recording (`Device::drawGeometry` 17 % of the flusher, of it the paint key 11 %): 0.5-0.8 µs
  per draw before any GPU command exists. JBR Vulkan writes vertices into an open command buffer; Direct3D 9 hands
  them to the driver. Java2D-level per op (benchGraphite, 2026-11-03): rect 271 against 137 ns (JBR Vulkan), LCD
  token 1 696 against 549 ns, image 1 810 ns against Direct3D 9's 457.
- Per recording: snap + insert (render passes encoded) + submit, about 25 % of the flusher; at least 40 µs on Vulkan,
  86 µs on Dawn, about 280 µs at the IDE's size. JBR Vulkan has no such phase.
- Dawn only: the submit replays every command into D3D11 (1.3 s against 0.3 s on Vulkan, s1) and DXGI's present.
- Present: the driver's present on both Vulkan pipelines (about 0.6 s a typing launch); Direct3D 9 does not wait.
- The backend's own layer: interpretation and batching (`SKQuadBatch` 13.5 %), LCD in two passes (Graphite gives
  clients no dual-source output), our glyph atlas.
- No longer: the extra window copy (gone with Swing's back buffer), per-flush recordings (held), batches broken by
  clips, colours, icons and kinds (batched). Draw counts no longer set the time (three reductions, no change).

**The judgment (see the hand-over reply of 2026-11-04 for the reasoning)**

- Strategic direction: a Skia-based renderer, Graphite on the GPU (Metal on macOS, where it beats JBR's Metal
  pipeline by a third in paint, ledger §11; native Vulkan first on Windows, Dawn/D3D11 as the fallback) and Skia's
  raster backend as the software path; JBR Vulkan kept working, not extended, until Skia's path is validated on more
  hardware and on Linux. Conditions before committing: the all-arms series below, a second GPU vendor, a fidelity-fair
  comparison, and a soak of present=cycle without Swing's back buffer in daily use.

**Next steps proposed**

1. One series with every arm on the current image (A0, R0, R1, R2, R2 without Swing's back buffer, A2, A7, A8), n = 5.
2. The same on the 5600G's Radeon iGPU (integrated GPUs are most of the installed base), and on an Intel laptop.
3. A fidelity-fair comparison: JBR Vulkan with LCD and AA honest, or Skia with greyscale text, to bound the shortcut share.
4. A soak of the new defaults (present=cycle, no Swing back buffer) in daily IDE use, for flicker.
5. A native profile after the last two changes; then the per-draw paint key and the passes per frame.
6. The raster backend profiled in the IDE (ETW), its small-op paths fixed, as the replacement of the loops.
