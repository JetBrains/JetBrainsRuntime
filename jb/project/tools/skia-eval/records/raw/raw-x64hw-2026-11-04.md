**Where Skia's time goes against the direct pipelines (ETW 2026-11-03, typing; the profile predates the last two changes)**

- Per draw: Graphite's recording (`Device::drawGeometry` 17 % of the flusher, of it the paint key 11 %): 0.5-0.8 µs
  per draw before any GPU command exists. JBR Vulkan writes vertices into an open command buffer; Direct3D 9 hands
  them to the driver. Java2D-level per op (benchGraphite, 2026-11-03): rect 271 against 137 ns (JBR Vulkan), LCD
  token 1 696 against 549 ns, image 1 810 ns against Direct3D 9's 457.
- Per recording: snap + insert (render passes encoded) + submit, about 25 % of the flusher; at least 40 µs on Vulkan,
  86 µs on Dawn, about 280 µs at the IDE's size. JBR Vulkan has no such phase.
- Dawn only: the submit replays every command into D3D11 (1.3 s against 0.3 s on Vulkan, s1) and DXGI's present.
- Present: the driver's present on both Vulkan pipelines (about 0.6 s a typing launch); Direct3D 9 does not wait.
- The backend's own layer: interpretation and batching (`SKQuadBatch` 13.5 %), LCD in two passes (Graphite gives
  clients no dual-source output), our glyph atlas.
- No longer: the extra window copy (gone with Swing's back buffer), per-flush recordings (held), batches broken by
  clips, colours, icons and kinds (batched). Draw counts no longer set the time (three reductions, no change).

**The judgment (see the hand-over reply of 2026-11-04 for the reasoning)**

- Strategic direction: a Skia-based renderer, Graphite on the GPU (Metal on macOS, where it beats JBR's Metal
  pipeline by a third in paint, ledger §11; native Vulkan first on Windows, Dawn/D3D11 as the fallback) and Skia's
  raster backend as the software path; JBR Vulkan kept working, not extended, until Skia's path is validated on more
  hardware and on Linux. Conditions before committing: the all-arms series below, a second GPU vendor, a fidelity-fair
  comparison, and a soak of present=cycle without Swing's back buffer in daily use.

**The EDT and the flush**

- Every Java2D GPU pipeline waits for its flush on the EDT: Skia's and Direct3D 9's `flushNow` hand the buffer to the
  flusher thread and wait for it (`SKRenderQueue.QueueFlusher.flushNow`: `while (needsFlush) wait()`), JBR Vulkan's
  runs the flush on the EDT. The GPU takes the pixel work off the EDT, not the command encoding: the flush wait (Skia
  /Vulkan 3.2-3.3 s a typing launch, a third of the EDT's paint) is EDT time today.
- Presents per second at most 64 (typing) and 71 (UI) on a 143 Hz panel: pacing presents to the refresh would save
  nothing here.

**The paint cycle's flush handed to the flusher without the EDT waiting (EXPERIMENT `sun.java2d.skia.asyncPresent=true`): EDT paint −25 % typing, −13 % UI**

- Prior art: OpenJDK's single-threaded rendering (Java 6, the OpenGL pipeline: one flusher thread, `flushNow` a
  synchronous hand-off, a 100 ms timeout flush) is the model every pipeline since copied (D3D, Metal, JBR Vulkan,
  this one); no asynchronous variant in OpenJDK. The cost of the synchronous wait is known (EDT stalls in
  `RenderQueue.ensureCapacity` → `OGLRenderQueue.flushNow`, FrostWire issue 1306, 2026-10).
- What the design relies on, checked: (1) the render-queue lock is the AWT lock, and today the flusher runs while
  the waiting EDT holds it for it, which keeps every native entry point and windowing operation apart from a flush;
  (2) callers keep `rq.getBuffer()` across `ensureCapacity` (RenderQueue's own documented pattern, `SKRenderQueue.sync`),
  so the buffer object cannot change; (3) data outside the buffer: software sources (BufferedImage rasters) flush
  synchronously right after they are queued ("the source raster is only guaranteed to stay valid for this call"),
  read-backs and disposals flush synchronously, glyph strikes and surfaces are kept alive by the queue's reference set,
  font strikes are freed through `flushAndInvokeNow`; (4) drawing entry points with arrays (`drawPoly`, `maskFill`,
  `drawShape`, Skia's text) run on the flusher through `flushAndInvokeNow`; (5) the flush's only call back into Java
  (frame counters) takes its own monitor only.
- Design: only the flush that ends a paint cycle (`SKSurfaceData.requestPresent`, most of a frame's work: the buffer
  is 6.4 MB and rarely fills) is handed off: its content is copied to a second buffer (`RenderBuffer.copyTo`, new) and
  the reference set goes with it; the flusher runs it while holding its monitor, so every other flush (`flushNow`,
  `flushAndInvokeNow`, sync, read-backs, software sources, disposals) and the next hand-off wait for it, in order.
  Natively, every JNI entry point that touches backend or platform state (the queue's, surface and config creation
  and disposal, context preparation, configuration, the bench) takes a recursive mutex (`SKBackend_EntryMutex`), since
  no one holds the AWT lock during a handed-off flush.
- `SkiaAsyncPresentTest` (a heavy frame drawn onto a window, the paint cycle's commit, then Toolkit.sync): red on the
  image before (commit 3-27 ms, the sync after it 0.03-0.14 ms), green after on Dawn and Vulkan (commit 0.04-0.07 ms,
  the sync 3-26 ms, the window showing every frame); without the property unchanged. jtreg with the property forced on

**Why the hand-off was not done before (the reading, for the ledger)**

- It changes two invariants of Java2D's single-threaded rendering: that a flush's work has run when `flushNow`
  returns (relaxed for the paint cycle's flush only, whose callers never rely on it), and that native rendering runs
  while someone holds the AWT lock (replaced by the backend's own mutex). No record of an attempt was found; reasons
  it was not done elsewhere: on X11 the AWT lock is also Xlib's, which the OpenGL pipeline's present needs; on Windows
  Direct3D 9 and the loops mix GDI drawing onto the same windows; RenderQueue's own comment records that separating its
  lock from the AWT lock caused deadlocks (6253009) and was judged too risky; queued ops point at Java memory and
  callers cache the buffer, safe only with a synchronous hand-off or the op-by-op audit above; and the old pipelines'
  flush cost (Direct3D 9: about 2 s a typing launch) did not invite it. What makes it possible here is the backend's
  shape, not Skia's API: all native state behind JNI entry points it owns, windows drawn only through its own textures
  and swap chains (no GDI, no Xlib), a flusher thread already. The work moves off the EDT; the flusher's CPU stays.

**The extra presents and recordings: a race in the hand-off, fixed (`runs/s22-*`, A8, n = 3 interleaved)**

- Cause: after `wait(100)` returned, the flusher took the AWT lock for a timeout flush (presentAll) without checking
  for a pending hand-off; a hand-off's notify woke it, the EDT had just released the lock, and the flusher presented
  every window drawn into since the last present, before the handed-off flush ran: an extra present and recording per
  such hand-off, and windows presented with mid-cycle content. Fixed (the timeout path skips while a hand-off is
  pending). Guard: `SkiaAsyncPresentTest` now counts presents (`awt.window.counters`) over 40 paint cycles that copy a
  software image mid-cycle: 80 presents with the race, 40 after, on Dawn and Vulkan. New counter: timeout flushes.

**Tests for the hand-off's risks, step 1: a hook that holds a hand-off in flight, and the deterministic cases (`SkiaHandOffTest`)**

- Hooks (system properties, tests only): `sun.java2d.skia.test.handOffDelay=ms` holds every hand-off in flight that long
  (the flusher sleeps holding its monitor, so whatever waits for the hand-off waits), `sun.java2d.skia.test.handOffError=N`
  fails the N-th hand-off with an injected InternalError.
- Cases, each with a hand-off held 60 ms, Dawn and Vulkan: `sources` (a cached image and an untracked image drawn, changed
  and drawn again, across hand-offs and within one paint cycle), `dispose` (VolatileImages released right after a frame
  that draws them, explicitly and by the Disposer), `strikes` (weakly referenced font strikes collected while their glyphs
  are in flight), `sync` (the sync after a commit waits 60-61 ms for the hand-off; 20 presents for 20 paint cycles),
  `error` (the injected failure reaches the next Toolkit.sync; later frames drawn).
- Found: the hand-off's error was lost (stored in the field the next synchronous flush resets): `error` red before
  ("did not reach the next sync point: null"), green after (thrown by the next flush that waits or hand-off).
- Mutation checks: the software source copy handed off instead of waited for turns `sources` red (the first copy showed
  the changed pixels) once the change happens within the paint cycle (with the change between cycles the case stayed
  green: only one hand-off is in flight, the commit waits for the earlier one; rewritten). The surface releases handed
  off instead of waited for leave `dispose` green: their ops stay in order, and what could go wrong is a use-after-free
  of native memory that pixels and crashes do not reveal; that needs an address sanitizer (MSVC `/fsanitize=address`),
  as do the strikes. The kit's runner now gives run lines with a long common prefix a log each.
